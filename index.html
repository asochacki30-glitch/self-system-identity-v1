Self as System — Geometric Circle

Rules

  • Slow, sustained movement draws smooth, curling lines and circular nodes — never straight edges.
  • Fast movement shatters the drawing into sharp, geometric fragments — straight shards and triangles.

Controls

Move your cursor slowly across the canvas to draw soft curves. Move quickly to break the drawing into sharp geometric fragments.

LIVE SYSTEM — RESPONDS TO CURSOR

v1 Reflection

Across my four tests, the two ideas I'm keeping are the sense of resistance under speed from Test 2, and the idea that patience is rewarded while carelessness is punished from Test 3. Rather than carrying those forward as a single fixed sigil shape, I rebuilt them as a drawing system: slow, deliberate movement earns soft, curling lines and circular nodes, while fast movement breaks that same drawing into sharp, geometric fragments. I cut the static circle-and-line sigil from Test 1 entirely, since it turned out to be the drawing itself, not a fixed symbol, that best expressed the system. I tightened the vignette from Test 4 by keeping it as a constant, quiet frame around the piece rather than something that changes on its own — its role is to hold focus on the drawing, not compete with it.

End of Build Reflection

What can it do now that the tests couldn't? In the tests, each behavior lived in its own isolated canvas reacting to its own input. Now a single input — cursor speed — governs one continuous drawing that can shift character entirely, from organic curves to sharp geometric shards, rather than four separate demonstrations sitting side by side.

What feels most "you" about the behavior? The contrast between the two states feels the most deliberate: care and patience produce something soft and organic, while speed and carelessness produce something sharp and broken. The system has an opinion about how it wants to be treated.

Where does it drift into generic or noisy? If the cursor moves fast for too long, the canvas can fill with shards and start to feel cluttered rather than intentional — right now nothing limits how much can accumulate, so restraint depends entirely on how the person chooses to move.

What will you change in v2? I want to add a limit or decay to the fragments specifically, so speed still fractures the drawing but doesn't let it pile up indefinitely — keeping the shatter feeling sharp and sudden rather than eventually becoming visual noise.
